Press a hotkey and talk. Your words type into any app — email, editor, chat, anywhere you can put a cursor.

## The loop

<Steps>
  <Step title="Put your cursor where you want text to appear">
    Any text field in any application.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Hold the Dictate shortcut and speak naturally">
    `Left Ctrl` + `Win` on Windows, `Option` + `Space` on macOS, `Ctrl` + `Space` on Linux by default.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Release">
    SpeakoFlow transcribes and inserts your words into the active app.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The overlay shows *Getting mic ready…* until the microphone actually starts delivering audio, then switches to a live waveform. That cue is driven by real audio frames rather than a fixed delay, so once the wave moves you know your words are being captured.

Voice-activity detection filters non-speech audio out of the recording, so brief pauses while you think won't add gaps.

<Tip>
  Talking is roughly **3× faster than typing** — around 150 words a minute spoken versus 45 typed. The speed adds up fast across a day of messages and notes.
</Tip>

## Recording behavior

*Settings → General → Recording → Recording behavior.*

- **Hold** (default) — records while the Dictate shortcut is held, and stops on release.
- **Tap** — one press starts recording, the next press stops it. This is how you record hands-free.

See [Hands-free recording](/dictation/hands-free) for the details, including how to end a hands-free recording.

<Note>
  A single recording is capped at 30 minutes. When it hits the cap, SpeakoFlow finalizes it automatically — stops, saves the audio, and transcribes — rather than recording indefinitely. Audio is only written to disk after a recording stops, so the cap guarantees a forgotten session still produces a transcript instead of losing everything.
</Note>

## Sounds and microphone

Start and stop cues are **off by default** — turn on *Audio feedback* in *Settings → General → Sounds* if you want them, then pick a **Feedback sound**: Default, Marimba, Pop, or Click, plus a **Custom** option that appears only if you've supplied your own sound files (see [General settings](/settings/general)). *Mute while recording* silences system audio for the duration of a recording, and is also off by default.

The microphone is chosen in *Settings → General → Recording*. Two related options live elsewhere:

- **Always-on microphone** (*Settings → Dictation → Output options*, off by default) keeps the mic warm so the first words are never clipped, at the cost of a little battery.
- **Clamshell microphone** (*Settings → Debug*) picks a different input for when a laptop lid is closed.
