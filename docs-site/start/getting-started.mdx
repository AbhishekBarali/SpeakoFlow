Install the app, run the three-step wizard, and dictate your first sentence. The whole thing takes a couple of minutes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install" icon="download">
    Download SpeakoFlow for your platform and install it. Full per-platform instructions, including the one-time macOS Terminal command, are on [Install SpeakoFlow](/start/install).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Grant permissions" icon="lock">
    On macOS and Windows the wizard opens with a permissions screen, because nothing else works until these are granted. It skips itself if you've already granted them, and it doesn't appear on Linux.

    - **Microphone access** (macOS and Windows) — required to hear your voice.
    - **Accessibility access** (macOS only) — required to type transcribed text into your applications.

    If you skip these, you'll be prompted again the next time SpeakoFlow starts and finds them missing. See [Troubleshooting](/reference/troubleshooting) if a permission gets stuck.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 1 — pick a voice model" icon="microphone">
    Choose a speech-to-text model. This is the part that turns your voice into text, and it runs entirely on your machine. Pick one now — you can switch anytime in *Settings → Dictation*. A fast English model or a real-time multilingual model are both good starting points.

    The download runs in the background, so you don't have to wait here. See [Transcription models](/models/transcription-models) for the full catalog.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2 — give it a brain (optional)" icon="brain">
    Optionally download a local model for the assistant and AI cleanup. Three Gemma 4 sizes are offered, and the badge is editorial rather than inferred from your hardware:

    - **Quick** (E2B) — responds fastest, less capable on complex requests.
    - **Balanced** (E4B, recommended) — the best conversational quality-to-latency tradeoff.
    - **Capable** (12B) — more capable, but noticeably slower.

    Tapping a card starts the download and immediately flips the footer to *Continue*, so you can move on while it finishes. The built-in assistant provider is only pointed at the model once the weights are actually on disk, which means a failed or cancelled download can't leave the assistant set to a model you don't have.

    *Skip for now* is always available. You can add or change this later under *Settings → Assistant*, including pointing at a local server or a cloud provider instead — see [Brain: local or cloud](/assistant/brain).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3 — you're ready" icon="keyboard">
    The last screen shows your two shortcuts as real keycaps and a status line for anything still downloading. Your hotkeys are already live at this point.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Say your first sentence" icon="comment">
    Open any text field — an email, an editor, a chat box — then hold your Dictate shortcut and talk. Let go, and your words type themselves. That's the whole loop.

    | Action | Windows | macOS | Linux |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Dictate | `Left Ctrl` + `Left Win` | `Option` + `Space` | `Ctrl` + `Space` |
    | Ask the assistant | `Left Ctrl` + `Left Alt` | `Option` + `Ctrl` + `Space` | `Ctrl` + `Alt` + `Space` |

    Every shortcut is rebindable in *Settings → General → Shortcuts*. The full list is in [Keyboard shortcuts](/start/keyboard-shortcuts).
  </Step>
</Steps>
