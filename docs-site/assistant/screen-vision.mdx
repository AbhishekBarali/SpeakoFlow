Ask about what is on your screen and the assistant answers with that context. It only looks when you let it.

Set it up on the _Assistant_ page, under _What it can do_. You need a vision-capable model. Most curated local models qualify (the catalog marks them "Vision"), as do the mainstream cloud multimodal models.

## Two switches

| Switch                          | What it covers                                     |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Screen vision for questions** | The quick ask: one question from the ask shortcut. |
| **Screen vision for calls**     | A hands-free call, for its whole length.           |

Both are **off** until you turn them on. They are separate because a call turns every sentence into a question, so you may be happy for a one-off question to look but not want the screen in reach for a whole conversation.

## How the assistant decides to look

Turning a switch on does not mean every question takes a screenshot. It means the assistant _may_ look, and it decides per message:

- **It looks** when you ask it to ("look at my screen", "can you see this?"), or when your question is about something on screen it has not been given as text, such as "what's this error?" or "reply to this email".
- **It does not look** for anything it can answer without seeing: general knowledge, writing, translation, maths, the time, reminders, or a follow-up about something already in the conversation.
- **Selected text counts as "this".** Select a sentence and ask "what does this mean?", and it answers from the selection instead of taking a screenshot. Ask it to look at the screen as well and it will.

It looks at most once per message. Every screenshot that goes to the model is shown in the conversation, so there is always a visible record of when the assistant looked.

<Warning>
  **macOS.** Screen vision needs macOS Screen Recording permission. Grant it in
  *System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording*, then restart
  SpeakoFlow for it to take effect.
</Warning>

## What actually gets sent

SpeakoFlow captures the monitor under your mouse cursor, so on a multi-monitor desk you get the screen you are actually working on.

Only a compact thumbnail is stored in the conversation and in [History](/personalize/history). The full-resolution frame is sent to the model once and never written to disk. Thumbnails show inline in the panel and can be clicked to enlarge.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="When the frame is grabbed for a voice question">
    *When I start, When I send. Default: When I start. Shown when screen vision for questions is on.*

    This only changes *when* the frame is taken, never whether it is sent. The frame waits on your machine, and is dropped at the end of the question if the assistant does not ask to look.

    - **When I start** (default). Grabs the frame the moment you press the hotkey, so it shows what you were looking at when you started talking. It also means that when the assistant does decide to look, the screenshot is already there.
    - **When I send**. Grabs it once you stop talking, so it shows the screen as it is when the question goes out.

    Typed questions capture when they are sent. A call captures only at the moment the assistant asks to look.

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How much image quality each provider gets">
    The frame is JPEG-compressed down to a budget picked from your provider, because "send the sharpest possible image" and "the request succeeds" are not the same goal.

    | Provider | Budget | Why |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Azure | Smallest | Azure's gateway rejects oversized request bodies outright. |
    | Built-in or a local server | Middle | Keeps on-screen text legible without spending a huge share of a small local context on vision tokens. |
    | Other cloud providers | Largest | Sharpest, so fine text, code, and error messages stay readable. |

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if your model cannot read images">
    The panel says so and names the problem rather than silently dropping the screenshot. Switch to a vision-capable model, or ask again with screen vision off.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
