Open the **Meetings** page from the sidebar and press **Start recording** before a call. SpeakoFlow records your microphone and your computer's audio as two separate streams, so it always knows which words were yours and which came from the other side.

No bot joins the meeting. It works with any app that uses your microphone: Zoom, Teams, Meet, Discord, Slack, a browser tab, or anything else.

## What you see while recording

A live transcript appears on the **Meetings** page as people speak. Your words are labelled **You** and the other side is labelled **Others**. After the call, speaker identification can split "Others" into Speaker 1, Speaker 2, and so on (see [Notes](/meetings/notes)).

Two level meters on the recorder card show whether both streams are capturing. If the system-audio meter stays flat, the other side's audio is not reaching the app. See the platform notes below.

While recording, you can **Pause** to stop the capture and **Resume** to pick it up again. Press **Stop** to end the recording. Stopping can take a moment on a long meeting while the last chunks are transcribed.

## Platform notes

### Windows

Windows captures the other side of the call through WASAPI loopback. No setup is needed.

**Offer to record calls.** On Windows, SpeakoFlow can notice when another app starts using your microphone and offer to record. It never starts on its own. You see a card with "You seem to be on a call. Record it?" and you accept or dismiss it. Turn this on under the **Offer to record calls** toggle on the **Meetings** page. If it guesses wrong, just dismiss the card.

A **floating indicator** option puts a small pill on screen while a meeting records. Click it for the live transcript. Turn it off to keep your screen clear; the recording stays on the Meetings page and you stop it from there.

### macOS

macOS gives apps no direct way to record the sound your computer plays. You need a virtual audio device in between.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install a loopback driver">
    Install [BlackHole](https://github.com/ExistentialAudio/BlackHole) or
    another virtual audio device.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a Multi-Output Device">
    Open Audio MIDI Setup, create a Multi-Output Device that sends sound to both
    your speakers (or headphones) and BlackHole, and set it as your system
    output.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record">
    SpeakoFlow can now capture the other side of the call through the BlackHole
    device. Your microphone is recorded either way.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Without this setup, the meeting records only your side. The Meetings page shows a notice when system audio is unavailable.

### Linux

Linux captures the other side through your PulseAudio or PipeWire monitor source. No extra setup is usually needed, but the monitor device must be available.

## Models and settings on the Meetings page

Below the recorder, the **Meetings** page shows two model pickers:

| Setting            | What it does                                                                                                     |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Transcribed by** | The model that writes the live transcript. This is your speech-to-text model, the same one dictation uses.       |
| **Notes by**       | The model that writes the notes, the title, and answers questions about a meeting. This is your assistant model. |

Change either by clicking the picker. They go to the same model pages as dictation and the assistant, so there is nothing extra to set up.

## Quitting mid-recording

If the app quits while a meeting is recording, SpeakoFlow saves the audio and transcript it has so far. The next time the app starts, it reconciles the interrupted meeting: the recording appears in the list with an **Interrupted** status, and whatever was transcribed before the quit is kept. The WAV files are written before the quit, so the audio is not lost.

## Call detection (Windows only)

Call detection asks the operating system whether another process is holding the microphone open right now. It does not check app names. A browser in a Meet call holds a capture session; the same browser playing a video does not. The detection fires a suggestion you accept or dismiss. It never starts recording on its own.

On macOS and Linux, call detection is not available. The Meetings page says so: "Call detection is only available on Windows so far. On this system, start a meeting yourself."
